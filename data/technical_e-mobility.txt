Electric Vehicles and Batteries Market Assessment - This LUMS project, assessed Pakistan's EV and battery market through over 100 interviews with industry and public sector representatives. It analysed the current and future market state, reviewed the Electric Vehicle Policy, and compared local EV policies with those of regional countries to identify best practices.
This project is a rapid assessment of the Electric Vehicles (EVs) and batteries market in Pakistan, conducted by LUMS. Over 100 interviews were conducted with individuals representing automobile manufacturers, automotive parts manufacturers, and public sector entities involved in governing the EV value chain in Pakistan. A Framework to Quantify the Degradation Modes of a Lithium-ion Cell - Battery degradation affects the performance and lifespan of lithium-ion batteries, crucial for electric vehicles and energy storage. This project studies key degradation modes—loss of active material, lithium inventory, and conduction efficiency—under various conditions. Insights will help extend battery life, improve performance, and support sustainable energy technologies.
Understanding battery degradation is crucial because it directly impacts the performance and lifespan of lithium-ion batteries, which power many everyday technologies, including electric vehicles (EVs). Although these batteries are rechargeable and offer high energy and power density, they degrade over time due to chemical changes that corrode the electrodes and decompose the electrolyte within the cell. This degradation results in reduced efficiency and capacity fade, affecting the reliability and longevity of the batteries. Addressing battery degradation can extend battery life, improve performance, and support sustainable energy solutions like EVs. Additionally, managing degradation is essential for controlling maintenance costs, optimizing resource use, and ensuring the environmental sustainability of energy storage systems. Thus, studying battery degradation is key to advancing clean energy technologies and promoting their widespread adoption. This project aims to tackle the critical challenge of battery degradation in sustainable energy. As batteries become increasingly important for powering applications such as electric vehicles and renewable energy storage, understanding how they degrade is essential for maximizing their lifespan and optimizing performance. Our investigation focuses on identifying the primary modes of battery degradation, specifically in three areas: loss of active material, conduction losses and loss of lithium inventory. By analysing data obtained from experiments, we aim to uncover the mechanisms driving battery degradation. Our study quantifies degradation modes at 80%, 50%, and 20% state of charge under dynamic load profiles and constant temperature. The insights gained from this research will be invaluable to researchers, industry professionals, and policymakers, helping them develop more durable and efficient batteries. Ultimately, this project contributes to the advancement of sustainable energy solutions by providing a deeper understanding of battery degradation, which is crucial for the continued growth and success of clean energy technologies.

Feasibility study for Actualization and Implementation of Electric Vehicle Policy in Punjab - Punjab has significant potential for electric vehicle (EV) growth, given its large population, road network, and increasing vehicle numbers. The transportation sector, dominated by fossil fuels, is the largest contributor to emissions in Pakistan, making EVs crucial for reducing pollution and meeting climate goals. The province aims to implement EV policies and infrastructure to accelerate adoption and address range anxiety.
Punjab holds significant potential for the growth of electric vehicles (EVs) and battery storage. As the most populous province in Pakistan, with a median age of 23 years, Punjab also boasts the largest road network in the country. Currently, there are around 21.9 million registered vehicles in the province, with 82% comprising two and three-wheelers, 9.5% passenger cars, and 4% buses, trucks, and other specialized vehicles. The automotive sector has experienced double-digit growth in recent years, driven by a young population and a relatively new network of highways and motorways. This trend is expected to continue over the next decade. The fossil fuel-dominated transportation sector in Pakistan is the largest contributor to harmful emissions, severely impacting the environment. Consequently, pollution levels have exceeded the safe limits set by the World Health Organization (WHO) and other agencies. As a signatory to the Paris Climate Agreement, Pakistan has pledged to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 20% by 2030. Reducing emissions from the transportation sector is crucial to meeting this commitment and achieving the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals. We believe that EVs can play a pivotal role in addressing the challenges faced by Pakistan's energy and transport sectors. EVs produce no tailpipe emissions and can also act as a flexible load by utilizing surplus generation capacity during off-peak hours. To support EV adoption, the National Electric Vehicle Policy (NEVP) was approved in 2019, offering incentives to EV users and manufacturers. However, developing DC fast-charging infrastructure on motorways and highways to alleviate 'range anxiety' remains a significant challenge. This report aims to guide the Government of Punjab in swiftly implementing the EV policy and accelerating EV adoption across the province.
Development of Bigdata Based Platform to Promote Electric Vehicles in Pakistan - This project will develop a big-data platform for swappable battery charging, focusing on 3-wheelers. It will collect data from vehicle components and vending machines via the CAN protocol and transmit it to a central server. This data will be used to provide real-time battery information to users via a mobile app, optimize charging station distribution, and inform policy for electric mobility.
In the proposed project, we aim to develop a big-data-based platform to facilitate swappable battery charging, particularly for 3-wheelers. The methodology includes extracting data from the battery, motor, and motor control unit in the vehicle via the CAN protocol. This data will be transmitted through cellular networks or Wi-Fi to a central server. Similarly, the battery vending machine at the swappable battery station will send real-time data about battery health, availability, and pricing. This information will be relayed via 4G or a similar network to the central server, where it will be processed to generate valuable insights. The processed data will be made available to EV users through a mobile application, allowing them to check nearby charging stations, battery pack availability, and the health of each battery pack. Additionally, the data at the central server will be utilized for various purposes, such as determining the optimal spatial distribution of charging stations, developing standards and specifications for swappable batteries, and refining a business model for swappable battery-based charging solutions. The insights gained from this project will also inform policy initiatives to support the mass adoption of electric mobility. This project represents an effort to create a unique charging solution tailored to meet local requirements.

Traversing the Path Towards Next Generation Electric Transportation in Pakistan, Models and Scenarios - This project reviews Pakistan's transportation sector, forecasts growth, and compares EV targets with other countries. It assesses electricity supply, economic challenges, and the impact of emissions and petroleum costs, concluding with policy recommendations for key government entities to support EV growth.
This project begins by providing an overview of Pakistan's transportation sector, detailing the number of vehicles and the length of the road network. It forecasts the growth of the sector up to 2025, including expected increases in vehicles and road infrastructure. The project then examines EV penetration targets in selected countries, particularly India, China, and Sri Lanka, offering a comparative perspective. Next, the project addresses Pakistan's electricity supply and demand, highlighting the short-term availability of surplus generation capacity to support EV growth. It also discusses the economic challenges Pakistan faces, including growing circular debt and increasing capacity payments in the energy sector. Additionally, the project forecasts the potential load on the grid due to EV adoption in the near term. The project also evaluates the impact of harmful emissions from the transportation sector on public health in Pakistan and projects the rising cost of petroleum imports if EV adoption remains minimal. It assesses the socio-economic costs of the current fossil fuel-based transportation system, both qualitatively and quantitatively. Through a case study, the project calculates the potential savings EVs can offer compared to their fossil fuel counterparts. Finally, the project identifies key government entities responsible for developing the EV value chain in Pakistan. It concludes with a set of policy recommendations for these entities, outlining their roles in fostering the growth of the EV ecosystem in the country.
Developing Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure Across Highways and Motorways of Pakistan - This project develops a plan for DC-fast charging infrastructure on Pakistan's highways, identifying 85 optimal locations, with 15 prioritized for initial development. A case study forecasts the need for 3 to 8 chargers at each of five sites on M-2 by 2030, depending on EV adoption rates. The preliminary business model suggests low charging tariffs to promote EV adoption and proposes a profit-sharing model with nearby businesses and the use of portable chargers for capacity optimization.
This project aims to guide the development of DC-fast charging infrastructure on Pakistan's highways and motorways. The initial phase involved collecting daily traffic flow data from December 2019 to October 2020 on major routes, including N-5, M-1, M-3, M-4, M-5, and M-9. Data for M-2 was estimated through various methods, including physical observations and Google traffic maps. Based on this data, 85 optimal locations for charging stations were identified, with 15 prioritized for initial development. It is proposed that deploying 10 DC-fast chargers at each of these locations would suffice for short-term inter-city charging needs, given the current limited EV presence in Pakistan. However, as EV adoption increases, more infrastructure will be needed at the identified sites. A case study forecasts the growth of the 4-wheeler segment on M-2 until 2030, considering three EV adoption scenarios: 1%, 2%, and 3% of the annual vehicle increase being EVs. The projected EV traffic during peak hours in 2030 could range from 75 to 230 vehicles. To meet these demands, 3 to 8 DC-fast chargers would be required at each of the five proposed charging stations on M-2, assuming each charger can serve two vehicles simultaneously. The study also assumes that urban charging infrastructure will be well-established. A preliminary business model was developed based on the 2% EV growth scenario. Establishing DC-fast charging stations involves significant capital expenditures, and to encourage EV adoption, charging tariffs must remain low, leading to a slow return on investment. However, charging stations could attract customers to nearby businesses, and a profit-sharing model is suggested where these businesses share a percentage of their profits with charging service providers. Additionally, truck-mounted portable chargers could optimize underutilized capacity during off-peak hours by shifting to high-demand areas as needed.
eMobility Research and Development Center, focuses on advancing electric vehicle technology in Pakistan through battery and motor testing. It supports the development of an indigenous EV market and runs a pilot project with 20 electric rickshaws in Multan, which shows significant energy and CO2 emission savings compared to traditional LPG rickshaws.
The LUMS e-Mobility Research and Development Center, is a dedicated electric vehicle battery and motor testing project designed to contribute to the advancement of electric vehicle technology in Pakistan. The research center aims to help combat climate crises in Pakistan by reducing carbon footprint by eliminating vehicular exhaust emissions, faster adoption of electric vehicles, and decreasing fossil fuel imports. The research center facilitates the building of an indigenous electric vehicle market by offering battery and motor testing to develop electric vehicle prototypes. The performance and safety of the individual components of the electric vehicle including battery, motor, BMS, drive train efficiency, and the complete vehicle can be tested at the eMobility R&D Center, which gives information about the performance parameters of the developed prototype. To get insights into the implementation of electric vehicle technology on the road, the eMobility R&D Center is conducting a pilot project in Multan consisting of 20 electric vehicle rickshaws for public and commercial use. This pilot project is based on swappable batteries that enable drivers to exchange depleted batteries for fully charged ones to eliminate the charge time anxiety. The pilot project is running for one year and the batteries and the performance of the complete drive train are being monitored remotely. The results show that the EVs in the pilot project achieve 70-80 km per battery charge, resulting in energy savings of 11.09 MWh/month as compared to traditional LPG rickshaws. The CO2 emissions have decreased by 1.74 MT compared to an equivalent number of traditional rickshaws, highlighting the role of EVs in combating climate change. The case study on electric vehicle rickshaws compared to LPG-based rickshaws results in 53% CO2 emission savings and 79% energy savings.

Pakistan, Swappable Battery-Based Network for Promoting Electric Vehicles (DPP Stage) - This project seeks to boost EV adoption in Pakistan by deploying 4,000 electric three-wheelers in Multan, supported by solar-powered charging station with battery-swapping points provided at various locations in the city, while promoting sustainable mobility and reducing emissions.
The project aims to boost electric vehicle (EV) adoption in Pakistan through a battery-swapping model, starting with an initial target of 4,000 electric three-wheelers to be deployed in Multan over five years. The charging station on the outskirts of the city will be solar powered and the batteries will be made available at various spots across the city, providing convenient battery-swapping access for local residents. This initiative seeks to promote sustainable urban mobility and reduce carbon emissions. Currently, the project’s technical, financial, and business models are under evaluation to ensure successful implementation and long-term sustainability of the project.
