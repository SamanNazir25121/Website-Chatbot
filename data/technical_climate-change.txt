Real-time Spatiotemporal Emission Mapping - South Asian countries, especially Pakistan and India, face severe air pollution, worsened by industrialization and vehicle emissions. Our project uses low-cost stationary and mobile sensors to provide a sustainable, affordable solution for monitoring air quality, addressing both spatial and temporal data challenges in urban areas.
South Asian countries, especially Pakistan and India, are experiencing severe air pollution. In Pakistan's metropolitan areas, air quality has deteriorated to hazardous levels due to industrialization, population growth, and emissions from vehicles and proliferation of industrial areas. Monitoring air quality is crucial for sustainable urban development. However, the lack of a reliable air quality monitoring system and data poses significant challenges in developing effective plans to improve air quality. Many countries in the region struggle with the high capital, operational, and maintenance costs associated with reference-grade air quality monitoring systems. Our project addresses this issue by utilizing a hybrid system of low-cost sensors to measure air quality. In addition to stationary sensors, we have developed portable sensors mounted on electric three-wheelers. This combination of mobile and stationary sensor networks provides both spatial and temporal variability in air quality readings across the city. This approach reduces the overall number of sensors needed and offers a sustainable model for the long-term viability of air quality monitoring. By integrating low-cost technology with innovative deployment methods, our project aims to create a more affordable and efficient way to monitor and improve air quality in urban areas.
Promoting 100% Renewable Charging for Sustainable Urban Mobility in Pakistan - This project aims to build a network of EV charging stations powered entirely by renewable energy in Pakistan. It seeks to reduce the carbon footprint of urban transport, promote EV adoption, and support the country's climate goals.
This project, aims to establish a charging infrastructure powered entirely by renewable energy to promote sustainable urban mobility in Pakistan. The initiative focuses on creating a network of charging stations for electric vehicles (EVs) using energy from solar, wind, or other clean sources. By ensuring that the charging infrastructure is 100% renewable, the project aims to significantly reduce the carbon footprint of urban transportation, supporting the country's climate goals. In addition to lowering emissions, the project seeks to encourage the adoption of electric vehicles by providing accessible and eco-friendly charging options, transforming Pakistan’s urban transport system and making it less reliant on fossil fuels.
Power Sector Improvement Activity - The program seeks to transform Pakistan's power sector over four years through focused interventions. Key goals include developing a competitive market, enhancing T&D infrastructure, and improving energy planning. It aims to reduce reliance on coal, optimize system utilization, and support EV adoption. The program also targets DISCO improvements and better integration of renewable energy.
The program is designed to bring about significant change and achieve project objectives through a well-prioritized selection of focused interventions. Over its four-year duration, the program aims to maximize impact by addressing key areas in Pakistan’s power sector, including wholesale market implementation, transmission and distribution (T&D) improvements, and grid load optimization. Key objectives include: Market Development: Establishing the capabilities needed for the Competitive Trading Bilateral Contract Market (CTBCM) to ensure the market operator can perform essential functions smoothly before, during, and after its commercial operations date (COD), Competitive Procurement: Implementing market-mandated competitive procurement frameworks and reducing electricity prices through competitive auctions for renewable energy (RE). This will shift Pakistan from its increasing reliance on coal to cleaner energy sources. T&D Upgrades: Enhancing the technical and operational capacities of Pakistan's T&D sector to accommodate market growth and support digitization across the sector. Energy Planning: Assisting the Energy Planning and Resource Center (EPRC) and the National Transmission and Dispatch Commission (NTDC) in adopting modeling capabilities for developing and updating Integrated Energy Plans (IEPs), Indicative Generation Capacity Expansion Plans (IGCEPs), and Transmission System Expansion Plans (TSEPs). DISCO Improvements: Supporting management and operational enhancements at selected distribution companies (DISCOs) to reduce commercial losses, improve revenues, enhance customer service, and strengthen power sector data security and cyber resilience. Generation Surpluses: Addressing seasonal generation surpluses to improve system utilization, increase electricity use, and support new applications such as electric vehicle (EV) adoption and higher productivity. System Optimization: Optimizing system utilization and economics through better integration of variable renewable energy (VRE), distributed generation (DG), and storage-based augmentation. Overall, the program is aimed at transforming Pakistan’s power sector for better efficiency, sustainability, and reliability.
Installation of the First Indoor Air Quality Monitor in Skardu - The installation of the first indoor air quality monitor in the 'Third Pole of the World' is a key advancement in environmental monitoring. It will provide real-time data on pollutants like PM2.5 and CO2, crucial for assessing indoor air quality and its impact on health. This data will aid research and support policy development for improving air quality in the region.
The implementation of the first indoor air quality monitor in the 'Third Pole of the World' marks a significant milestone for environmental monitoring in this unique region. Known as the Third Pole, it faces increasing environmental challenges, including air pollution. The new indoor air quality monitor will provide real-time data on air pollutants such as PM2.5 and CO2, enabling more precise monitoring of indoor environments. This is especially important in areas where indoor air pollution can have a direct impact on the health of inhabitants. The monitor will support research on the links between air quality, human health, and environmental sustainability, helping policymakers to develop strategies needed for improving air quality in the region.
Capacity Building on Indoor Air Quality - This initiative targets poor indoor air quality in marginalized communities by introducing sustainable heating and cooking solutions and promoting education. It focuses on replacing harmful traditional cooking methods with clean technologies like efficient cookstoves and solar heaters. The program also emphasizes educating residents about air quality and proper use of these alternatives to improve health and create a healthier living environment.
This initiative aims to improve poor indoor air quality in marginalized communities by introducing sustainable heating and cooking solutions, while also focusing on education and awareness. Many of these lower income communities rely on traditional cooking methods, such as open fires or inefficient stoves, which release harmful pollutants like particulate matter and carbon monoxide, contributing to health issues such as respiratory diseases. The project seeks to promote cleaner, more energy-efficient technologies such as clean cookstoves or solar-powered heating, which can significantly reduce indoor air pollution. Alongside these technical solutions, another important component of the initiative is education, ensuring community members understand the dangers of poor air quality and the benefits of adopting sustainable alternatives. By raising awareness and providing training on proper use and maintenance, the program empowers communities to make informed decisions, leading to a healthier living environment.
